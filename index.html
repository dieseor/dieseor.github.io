Diego Serrano Ortega

I am a PhD candidate in Statistics for Data Science at the Department of Statistics of Universidad Carlos III de Madrid, and a member of the NICDA research group (Nonparametric Inference for Complex Data and its Applications). My thesis, Topics on random forests in metric spaces, is supervised by Eduardo García-Portugués and Marco Raiola.

I read Mathematics at Universidad Autónoma de Madrid (BSc, 2018–2022) and Statistics for Data Science at Universidad Carlos III de Madrid (MSc, 2022–2023). I have devoted myself to research and teaching since October 2023.

Research

I work on inference for random objects: data valued in a general metric space rather than a Euclidean one — distributions, covariance matrices, curves, directions, shapes. Only a distance is assumed, so the tools apply across very different kinds of data, at the price of having to redefine very basic notions such as the mean or the variance. My thesis envisages an application of metric space data analysis to aeroacoustics, since the data used in this area — flow fields and acoustic curves — is often complex.

In the first work of my thesis we introduced out-of-bag prediction balls: uncertainty regions for the prediction of any bagging regression model with responses in a metric space, with asymptotic coverage guarantees. More recently, together with Ingrid Van Keilegom (KU Leuven) and Eduardo García-Portugués (UC3M), I have been working on a goodness-of-fit framework for distributions on separable metric spaces, built on distance profiles.

Research interests

  • Goodness-of-fit testing
